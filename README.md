In this reproduction, we implemented the core LoMix method for medical image segmentation and adapted it to the Breast Ultrasound Images (BUSI) dataset.
We prepared the dataset by selecting the 647 benign and malignant lesion images, generating binary segmentation masks, resizing the images and masks to 256×256, and dividing the data into training, validation, and testing sets. Instead of the PVT-EMCAD-B2 backbone used in the original BUSI experiment, we adapted LoMix to a compact U-Net architecture to suit our available computational resources. We implemented the LoMix mechanism to combine multi-scale decoder predictions using different fusion operations and learnable weights during training. The implemented model will subsequently be evaluated and compared with standard single-output supervision and deep supervision using Dice and IoU metrics.

Section 1 - Preprocessing

The paper evaluates BUSI as one of its segmentation benchmarks, using the dataset of Al-Dhabyani et al. (780 images: 437 benign, 210 malignant, 133 normal). Only the 647 benign and malignant lesion images contain an actual lesion to segment, so the normal class is excluded; we follow this same exclusion.

Each raw image is matched with its mask file or files. Some images have more than one mask because a single scan can contain several distinct lesion regions; the paper does not state how it handles this. We combine all masks for an image into one binary mask with a pixel-wise logical OR, so a pixel marked as lesion in any mask becomes lesion in the final mask. This gives one training sample per image and keeps the total at 647.

The paper resizes BUSI images to 256x256 pixels, and we match this. Images use area interpolation (smooths grayscale ultrasound data), while masks use nearest-neighbor interpolation to preserve hard binary edges instead of introducing gray values along boundaries. The paper does not specify its interpolation choices, so this follows standard practice.

We replicate the paper's 80/10/10 train/validation/test split on our 647-image subset with a fixed seed (42) for reproducibility, and apply no augmentation, matching the paper; its BUSI batch size is 16.

Section 2 - Training

The training pipeline comprises unet_lomix.py (network), lomix.py (LoMix loss module), dataset_busi.py (data loader), and train_busi.py (training loop).

unet_lomix.py is a compact U-Net (classic DoubleConv, max-pool, and skip-connection design) with one 1x1 prediction head after each of its four decoder stages. It therefore emits four logit maps at different resolutions, from a 32x32 coarse map up to the final 256x256 prediction; the coarse maps are bilinearly upsampled so all four align pixel-by-pixel, and forward() returns them coarse to fine. This matches the contract of the paper's EMCADNet, which likewise hands four full-resolution logit maps (upsampled by x4/x8/x16/x32) to the LoMix loss.

The substantive difference is architectural: the paper uses a pretrained PVT-V2-B2 transformer encoder with the EMCAD multi-scale attention decoder, while our U-Net is fully convolutional and trained from scratch. Since LoMix is a training-time supervision method that works on any U-shaped network emitting several decoder-stage predictions, our adaptation is valid. Our absolute scores should not be compared with the paper's 80.25-81.32% DICE range for PVT-EMCAD-B2 + LoMix, but the relative gain of LoMix over single-output and deep-supervision baselines remains a meaningful comparison.


LoMix Module (lomix.py)


This file implements the CombinatorialMutationsLossModule, our reproduction of the LoMix supervision mechanism adapted for binary breast lesion segmentation on the BUSI dataset. Given a list of decoder output maps from our U-Net, one prediction per decoder stage, the module generates every possible combination of two or more of these maps and fuses each combination using one of several operators. Addition sums the selected maps element-wise, acting like a logical OR that highlights a pixel if any stage predicts it strongly. Multiplication takes the element-wise product instead, acting like a logical AND that only stays confident where all selected stages agree. Concatenation stacks the maps along the channel dimension and passes them through a learned 1 by 1 convolution, letting the network find its own best linear combination rather than using a fixed rule. Weighted fusion, handled by the separate WeightedFusion submodule, learns a per-pixel importance score for each prediction in the subset, normalizes these scores with softmax, and takes their weighted sum, so it can trust different decoder stages more or less at different spatial locations. Every original prediction and every fused combination is then assigned its own learnable scalar weight controlling how much its loss contributes to training, letting the network learn which predictions are actually useful.

Our implementation follows the core structure of the original LoMix method closely in several respects. Like the paper, we generate fused logits from every non-trivial subset of decoder stage predictions, meaning every combination of two or more original predictions, rather than only combining adjacent stages or fixed pairs. We use a Softplus transformation to convert raw learnable parameters into strictly positive loss weights, matching the paper's justification that Softplus avoids the sum-to-one constraint of Softmax and produces smoother gradients than ReLU or sigmoid alternatives. Every original prediction and every fused prediction receives its own independent learnable weight, and all of these weights are optimized jointly with the network parameters through standard backpropagation, exactly as described in the paper's training algorithm. Our concatenation operator also matches the paper's design, using a learned 1 by 1 convolution to combine channel-stacked predictions rather than a fixed linear combination. Our weighted fusion operator mirrors the paper's attention-weighted fusion equation, computing a per-pixel attention score for each prediction in a subset and normalizing these scores using softmax before summing. Finally, our loss combination formula uses the same 0.3 and 0.7 weighting between the two loss components that the paper specifies for its cross-entropy and DICE terms on the Synapse and ACDC datasets.


The most significant difference is that the paper's four fusion operators are addition, multiplication, concatenation, and attention-weighted fusion, while our implementation defines seven possible operators by default: addition, subtraction, multiplication, concatenation, weighted fusion, average, and max. Subtraction, average, and max are not described anywhere in the paper and are extensions we added ourselves. However, in our actual training test script, we restrict the active operator set to only add, mul, wf, and concat, meaning that in practice we do use the same four operators as the paper, and the additional three exist in the code as optional but currently unused extensions. Another key difference is in the loss function itself. The paper specifies that for multi-class datasets like Synapse and ACDC, it uses a weighted combination of cross-entropy and DICE loss with weights of 0.3 and 0.7. However, for the BUSI dataset specifically, along with ISIC2018 and the polyp datasets, the paper states it instead uses a hybrid weighted binary cross-entropy loss combined with a weighted Intersection over Union loss at a 1 to 1 ratio, which is a different loss formula entirely from the one used for the multi-class datasets. Our implementation does not replicate this BUSI-specific loss. Instead, we adapted the general cross-entropy and DICE formula from the multi-class case by simply substituting a binary cross-entropy loss for the standard cross-entropy loss, while keeping the same 0.3 and 0.7 weighting and using DICE rather than IoU as the second loss term. This means our loss formula is a hybrid of the paper's two different loss schemes rather than an exact reproduction of either one. Additionally, the paper does not specify how learnable weight parameters are initialized, whereas we explicitly initialize every raw weight parameter to zero before the Softplus transformation, which produces an initial weight of approximately 0.693 for every original and fused prediction rather than a value of exactly one or zero.




Dataset Loading and Training (dataset_busi.py and train_busi.py)

dataset_busi.py defines BUSIDataset, a PyTorch Dataset class that loads our preprocessed BUSI images and masks for a given split (train, val, or test). For each sample, it reads the grayscale image and its corresponding mask from disk, normalizes the image to a 0 to 1 floating point range and adds a channel dimension so it matches the shape PyTorch models expect, and converts the mask into a strict binary 0 or 1 array using a threshold of 127. Both are then converted into PyTorch tensors and returned as an image, mask pair.

train_busi.py is our full training loop. It loads the training and validation splits using BUSIDataset and wraps them in DataLoader objects with a batch size of 4, shuffling the training set but not the validation set. It then builds our U-Net model and our CombinatorialMutationsLossModule from lomix.py, using the same four operators, add, mul, weighted fusion, and concat, that we use elsewhere. Two loss functions are defined: a standard BCEWithLogitsLoss for binary cross-entropy, and a custom DiceLoss class that converts raw logits into probabilities using sigmoid, flattens each image, computes the overlap between prediction and target relative to their combined size, and returns 1 minus that overlap score as the loss. A single Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 1e-4 is used to update both the U-Net's parameters and LoMix's learnable fusion weights together, since they are combined into one parameter list.

The training loop itself runs for a set number of epochs. In each epoch, every training batch goes through the same sequence: move the images and masks to the selected device, add a channel dimension to the mask so its shape matches the model's four-dimensional prediction output, clear old gradients, run the images through the U-Net to get four decoder-stage predictions, pass those predictions along with the masks and both loss functions into the LoMix module to get a single combined loss value, backpropagate that loss, and step the optimizer. After all training batches are processed, the average training loss for the epoch is printed. The model then switches to evaluation mode and runs the same forward pass and loss calculation on the validation set, but wrapped in torch.no_grad() so no gradients are computed and no weights are updated. After every epoch, a checkpoint containing the model weights, the LoMix module's weights, the optimizer state, and both the training and validation loss is saved to a numbered file. If the current epoch's validation loss is the best seen so far, an additional copy of that checkpoint is saved separately as the best model.


Our overall training structure mirrors the general LoMix training procedure described in the paper's Algorithm 1. Each training step involves running the batch through the encoder-decoder network to get multi-stage logits, generating the combinatorial mutations from those logits, computing a weighted loss across both the original and mutated predictions, and updating the network parameters and the LoMix weight parameters together in a single optimizer step, exactly as the pseudocode describes. We also follow the paper's stated choice for the BUSI dataset of not applying data augmentation, consistent with what we already implemented in preprocessing. Our per-output loss also follows the same general two-term structure the paper uses, combining a classification-style loss with a DICE-based loss and weighting the two terms, rather than using either loss in isolation.


Several details in our training setup diverge from what the paper specifies. The paper reports training BUSI for 200 epochs with a batch size of 16, whereas our script uses a batch size of 4 and only 3 epochs, since we adapted the pipeline for local testing and available compute rather than the full-scale runs described in the paper. The paper uses the AdamW optimizer with a weight decay of 1e-4, while we use plain Adam with no weight decay specified, meaning our optimizer does not apply the same L2 regularization behavior. The paper's loss formula for BUSI specifically is a hybrid weighted binary cross-entropy loss combined with a weighted IoU loss, while our DiceLoss implementation computes a DICE coefficient rather than an IoU score, and our BCE term is unweighted. The paper also states that for BUSI, the final segmentation output is produced by summing the predictions from all four decoder stages, rather than using only the final stage as is done for the multi-organ datasets.
